The Big Question

“How Big is the Universe?”

This Online Treasure Hunt (OTH) guides you on a visual and quantitative journey across spatial scales—from our immediate solar neighborhood to the edge of the observable universe. You will act as cosmic cartographers and explorers, analyzing digital media, scientific interactive maps, and observational data to grasp the mind-boggling dimensions of space. By exploring 9 curated offline and online resources across 3 critical inquiry saturation points, you will build the mathematical, physical, and observational evidence to explain why light traveling for 13.8 billion years spans an observable diameter of ~93 billion light-years.

Student Learning Outcomes

  • Differentiate Spatial Horizons: Distinguish between the Observable Universe (~93 Gly diameter) and the Unobservable Universe.
  • Evaluate Cosmic Units: Transition fluently between Astronomical Units (AU), Light-Years (ly), Parsecs (pc), and Megaparsecs (Mpc).
  • Explain Metric Expansion: Demonstrate how Hubble's Law (v = H0 x d) causes comoving distances to exceed simple light travel time (c x t).
  • Analyze Structural Hierarchy: Map space from the Solar System to the Local Group, Laniakea Supercluster, and the Cosmic Web.

Target Concept & Master Synthesis Answer: "How Big Is the Universe?"

Target Concept Statement

"The observable universe is a sphere centered on the observer with a diameter of approximately 93 billion light-years (radius of ~46.5 billion light-years). Although light from the cosmic microwave background has traveled for 13.8 billion years ($t = 13.8\text{ Gy}$), the metric expansion of space during that transit time has stretched the comoving distance to those emission points. Beyond this particle horizon lies the unobservable universe, which inflation models suggest may be infinite or vastly larger."

This master key synthesizes evidence across all 9 video resources and 3 critical inquiry saturation points to provide the canonical answer to "How Big Is the Universe?"

1. Solar System to Stellar Baseline (Connected to Q1 & Resources 1.1, 1.2, 1.3) Planetary & Galactic Baseline

Standard distance units like kilometers (1 AU is approx 149.6 x 106 km; 1 ly is approx 9.46 x 1012 km) become mathematically impractical beyond planetary scales. Our Solar System spans out to the Oort Cloud (100,000 AU or 1.6 ly). Proxima Centauri lies 4.24 ly (1.30 pc) away, while our host galaxy—the Milky Way—spans an estimated 100,000 to 180,000 light-years in diameter, housing 100 to 400 billion stars.
